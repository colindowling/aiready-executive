The part nobody says out loud

You know you need to lead this. That's not the problem.

  • 1

    The board asked whether you had AI handled. You said yes, because the answer is yes — you're going to handle it. That part was straightforward.

  • 2

    Then you sat in a meeting where your CTO and a vendor talked for forty minutes, and you took notes instead of making a decision. Not because you couldn't follow it. Because you couldn't tell which parts mattered.

  • 3

    And here's the one you haven't said to anyone: somebody two levels below you is becoming the person this company listens to on AI. You can feel the room tilting. Once strategy moves to whoever can explain it, it doesn't move back on its own.

  • 4

    You don't need to learn to build any of it. You need to be the one who can tell a good answer from a confident one, ask the question that ends the hand-waving, and say where this company is going with it. That's a different skill than engineering, and it takes far less time to acquire than you think.

Leading means knowing what you're talking about. It does not mean knowing how to build it. Those get confused constantly, and the confusion is what keeps capable executives quiet in their own meetings.
