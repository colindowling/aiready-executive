Why Your Team's AI Answers Are Generic, and How Context Fixes It

Long AI chats lose context and return generic answers. Here's how executives can help teams give AI the context it needs to produce useful work.

November 1, 2026 · Colin Dowling

Key takeaways

  • Long, linear AI chats get confused and drift toward generic answers.
  • AI output is only as specific as the context it's given.
  • Build reusable context: company facts, audience, standards and examples.
  • Start fresh, focused conversations for each task instead of one endless thread.

Your team's AI answers are generic because the AI doesn't have enough context, and long, winding chats make it worse as earlier details get lost. Give it focused, reusable context about your company, audience and standards, and start a fresh conversation for each task.

A founder I spoke with recently described the pain he saw early on: you're stuck in a linear chat, the chat becomes confused, and you're having to scroll back up to find what you said.

Most people who use AI at work have felt that. It's also one of the main reasons executives hear "AI isn't that useful" from their teams.

Generic in, generic out

AI tools know a lot in general and nothing about your business in particular. When someone asks for "a proposal for our new client" with no other detail, the tool fills the gaps with general knowledge. The result reads fine and could have been written for anyone.

The quality of the output depends heavily on the context in the input.

Why long chats drift

People tend to keep one long conversation going for everything. Over time:

  • Earlier details get mixed with later ones.
  • Topics blend together.
  • The tool loses track of what matters most.

The answers get less specific, and people conclude the tool isn't very good.

Give AI the context it needs

A useful request includes:

  • Who you are. The company, the team, the role.
  • Who it's for. The audience and what they care about.
  • The goal. What the output needs to achieve.
  • Constraints. Length, tone, what to avoid.
  • An example. What good looks like.

That takes a minute to write and changes the result dramatically.

Build reusable context

Writing the same context every time is tedious, so people skip it. Make it easy:

  1. Create a company context document. Facts, messaging, audience, tone.
  2. Add examples. Good past work the AI can learn from.
  3. Store it where people can reuse it. In a shared project or approved tool.
  4. Keep it current. Update when things change.

This is one of the highest-return AI investments a company can make, and it costs almost nothing.

Keep conversations focused

Encourage teams to start a new conversation for each distinct task, restating the key context. Shorter, focused threads produce better answers than one endless chat.

A before-and-after example

Compare two requests. The first: "Write a follow-up email to a prospect." The second: "Write a short follow-up to a finance director at a mid-size manufacturer who asked about implementation time on our call. Keep it under 120 words, plain and friendly, and propose two meeting times." The second takes thirty seconds longer and produces something usable.

Make it a company capability

Individual tricks only go so far. When context is shared across the company, everyone's AI work improves at once, and quality becomes more consistent.

Signs your team has a context problem

  • People say AI output "needs so much editing it's not worth it."
  • Everyone writes their own prompts from scratch, every time.
  • One long chat thread is used for weeks across unrelated tasks.
  • AI drafts never mention your actual products, customers or terms.

What to do this week

Write a one-page company context document: who you are, who you serve, what you sell, how you talk and two examples of good work. Share it with the team and ask them to paste it in at the start of their next ten AI tasks. Then ask whether the output improved. It almost always does, and it's the cheapest AI improvement you'll make this year.

Common mistakes

  • Blaming the model first. Context is usually the gap.
  • Asking for everything in one prompt. Break work into focused tasks.
  • Letting context documents go stale. Update them when products or messaging change.
  • Keeping good prompts private. Share what works across teams.

Who should own this

Shared company context only works if someone keeps it current. Name one executive as the owner, usually the COO, CMO or an AI sponsor. Give them a clear goal, the authority to make the call and a regular slot to report back to the leadership team. Without a single owner, this becomes everyone's concern and no one's job, and it drifts until something goes wrong. With one, the questions above have someone accountable for answering them, and the board has one person to ask.

Questions

Why does ChatGPT give generic answers?

Usually because it lacks context. Without specifics about your company, audience, goals and standards, AI falls back on general knowledge and produces generic output. Long conversations can also drift, as earlier details get lost or mixed. Clear, focused context at the start of each task produces much more useful answers.

How do I give AI better context?

Tell it who you are, who the work is for, what you're trying to achieve, what constraints apply and what good looks like, ideally with an example. Provide relevant documents or data. Keep reusable context in a saved document or project so you don't start from scratch each time.

Why do long AI conversations get worse over time?

As a chat grows, the model has more to keep track of, and earlier details can be weighted less or confused with later ones. Topics blend together. Starting a new, focused conversation for each distinct task, with the key context restated, usually gives better results.

How can companies help employees use AI more effectively?

Provide shared context libraries with company facts, messaging, audience definitions and examples. Teach people to structure requests clearly and break work into focused tasks. Share good prompts and results across teams. Set up approved tools that can securely access relevant company information.
