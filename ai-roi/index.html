ROI

Why your AI projects don't have demonstrable ROI.

The spend is real. The slide that says it paid for itself is not. A pilot that stalled is not a technology mystery. It is a leadership problem: nobody agreed what “worked” meant before the tool was bought.

This page is for a non-technical executive at an operating company — manufacturing, logistics, engineering and construction — who is accountable for that spend and cannot yet defend it.

An AI ROI calculator is not on this page

How to measure AI ROI is the question. A calculator that only multiplies “hours saved” by a wage is a brochure, and this URL does not pretend to be one. A working calculator is required later, before this page can compete as a tool. Until that exists, the useful version would force four answers before it showed a number:

  • What decision changes if this works, and who owns that decision?
  • What is already being spent — licenses, pilots, contractors — and what did each one produce?
  • What stops if this is adopted: a queue, a handoff, a report someone still rebuilds by hand?
  • What would you refuse, even if a vendor demo looks finished?

Do not treat any figure on a future calculator as a fee for this engagement. Pricing on this site is unsettled. See the cost draft.

An AI pilot failed. What now.

Stop adding another tool to prove the last one was not a waste. Name the outcome that was supposed to move, whether anyone could measure it, and whether you could have challenged the proposal at the time. If you could not, the next purchase will look the same.

You do not need to learn to build the system. You need a position you can say out loud when your team, or a vendor, pushes the next one.
