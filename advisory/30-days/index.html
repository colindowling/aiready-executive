The month

In 30 days you can lead the conversation.

Not build the system. Not sit a course with your staff. One executive, in private, for a month. The point is a written AI position for this business: what is worth doing, what to refuse, and what you will tell the board.

You do not need to learn to code. That is not a footnote.

A proposed four weeks

Proposed only. Do not put this in a proposal until Colin signs the scope.

  • Week 1. How the technology actually works, without the math. You can follow a technical conversation and ask the next question.
  • Week 2. Where it creates value and where it does not. You can tell a real use case from a vendor demo.
  • Week 3. What it costs, what it breaks, what it risks. You can read a proposal and question the ROI claim.
  • Week 4. Governance, sequence, who to hire, and what to say to the board. You can set direction and hold the team to it.

The brief imagines four working sessions plus time between them, and a written artifact you own. “Four sessions, not a month on your calendar” is the answer to not having a month. It is not yet an offer.

Taught as an operator's work, not a faculty course. Credentials as published: 3× VP of Sales, 2× founder. About Colin Dowling.

Who it is for

A non-technical CEO, president, COO, or division leader at a company of roughly 200 to 1,000 people, often in manufacturing, logistics, or engineering and construction. Big enough that the board asks. Small enough that there is no internal AI function to hide behind.

It is not for a chief technology officer who already has the vocabulary. It is not a workshop for the team. Privacy is part of the product: one to one, nothing published, no cohort.
