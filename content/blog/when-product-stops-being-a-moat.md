When AI makes building software fast, cheap and good, the product alone stops being much of a moat. Features get easier to copy. What's left is the relationship with your audience: the customers who trust you, the people who know your name and the knowledge your team has built over years.

A technology leader I talked with recently asked the question directly. In a world where coding can be done quickly and with high quality through AI, what is left? His answer: the relationship with your audience.

I tend to agree, and I think most leadership teams haven't fully priced it in yet.

## Why features protect less than they used to

For a long time, building good software was hard and slow. That difficulty was a moat on its own. If you had a product that worked, a competitor needed a team, money and time to catch up.

AI shrinks that gap. A smaller team can now build in weeks what used to take quarters. That doesn't make your product worthless. It means a feature lead is shorter-lived than it used to be.

There's a startup graveyard of engineers who built something great and assumed people would just come. AI is going to make that graveyard bigger.

## The tension for executives

On the one hand, the product still has to be good. Nobody stays with a tool that doesn't work, no matter how much they like you.

On the other hand, if your whole strategy rests on being the best product, you're betting on a lead that's getting easier to close.

Where I land: keep the product strong, but stop treating it as the whole moat.

## What still holds up

The advantages that are hard to copy with AI tend to share one trait. They take time and trust to build.

- **Customer relationships.** The history between your team and your customers. Who they call when something breaks.
- **An audience.** People who know your name, read what you publish and think of you first when the problem shows up.
- **Distribution.** The channels that reliably bring buyers to you, from search to partners to referrals.
- **What you uniquely know.** The judgment your team has built from years of real customer work, which no model has seen.

A competitor with good AI tools can rebuild your features. They can't rebuild ten years of customers who trust you.

## A simple audit for the leadership team

Bring this to your next leadership meeting. List your company's top five competitive advantages. For each one, ask: could a well-funded competitor using AI rebuild this in a quarter?

Be honest. Most teams find at least one or two advantages they've been counting on that are more fragile than they thought. Those aren't reasons to panic. They're reasons to shift where the next dollar goes.

## A worked example

Picture a software company whose pitch is "the most complete feature set in the category." A newer competitor uses AI to ship a credible version of most of those features in a few months, at a lower price.

The company that wins that fight isn't the one that races to add more features. It's the one whose customers say, "We know this team. They understand our business. We're not switching." That company spent the last few years building relationships, publishing what it knows and staying close to its buyers. The features bought it time. The relationships kept the customers.

## Signs your moat is thinner than you think

- Your sales team's main pitch is a feature comparison chart.
- Customers describe you by what the product does, not by who they work with.
- You couldn't name the people at your top ten accounts who would fight to keep you.

## Who should own this

This is a CEO-level question, not a product roadmap item. Name one executive to own the moat review, usually the CEO or the leader closest to customers, and give them a regular slot with the leadership team. Their job is to track which advantages are getting easier to copy and to make sure investment follows the ones that aren't. Without an owner, the conversation happens once at an offsite and then fades.

## Common mistakes

- **Racing on features alone.** You're competing on the ground where AI helps your competitor most.
- **Cutting customer-facing people to fund AI.** You may be trimming the exact moat you'll need.
- **Treating audience-building as optional marketing.** It's strategy now.

## What to do this week

Run the five-advantage audit with your leadership team. Mark each advantage as easy or hard for an AI-equipped competitor to copy. Pick one hard-to-copy advantage and decide what you'll invest in it over the next 90 days.
